For designers · working with Claude

You don't need the right words. You need the real ones.

Claude is not a search box, and it is not a junior who needs a perfect brief. It works best when you talk the way you'd talk to someone sitting next to you. Five habits cover almost everything.

Show

Paste the picture instead of describing it

Claude can see. A screenshot of the actual screen, a reference you like, a photo of a sketch on paper — all of it works, and all of it beats a paragraph trying to explain the same thing.

Here's how it looks now. [paste] Here's what I want. [paste] Get the cards closer together.

Point

Say where, not just what

"Make the button bigger" could be any of forty buttons. Name the page and the part. You don't need file names — the words you'd use out loud are enough.

On the pricing page, in the plan cards — make the price bigger than the plan name.

Ask first

Get options before anything is built

When you don't know what you want yet, don't make Claude guess — it will guess, and then you're reacting to one answer instead of choosing between several. Say don't change anything out loud; that's the part that stops it from building.

Give me 3 options for the hero layout. Don't change anything yet.

One thing

Change one thing at a time

Five requests in one message get you five changes at once, and when something looks off you won't know which one did it. Small steps also mean you can say "go back" and only lose a minute.

Just the spacing for now — leave the colors alone.

Symptom

Describe what's wrong, not how to fix it

You don't have to know the cause. Say what you're seeing and Claude will find it — and if you guess at a fix, you can send it looking in the wrong place.

The last card overlaps the black section underneath it when I scroll.

If you remember one thing

You can always stop and say "wait — explain what you just did, in plain terms." Asking is free, and it's how you stay in charge of the work.
